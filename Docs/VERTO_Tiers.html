VERTO — Free vs Premium, decided at the command/flag level

Every command and flag, sorted into free or premium, with the reason. Companion to VERTO_Hosted (the cloud tier) and VERTO_Surfaces (the full CLI). The open-core split is architectural: free = runs on your machine; premium = needs our servers; the gate is free forever.

0The one rule (TL;DR)

Free = everything that runs entirely on your machine.

Premium = capabilities that need VERTO's servers.

The gate — the proof a change is correct-AND-faster — is free forever.

That's the whole policy. Almost the entire current CLI is free, because almost all of it runs locally. Premium is additive hosted value, not a lock on what already works.

1Four principles (the why behind the rule)

P1 — Never paywall the gate. The verification (differential test + sanitizers + Pareto + metamorphic) is the product's trust. A paywalled proof is worthless — a user who can't verify for free has no reason to believe us, and every competitor gives basic checks away. The gate must also be inspectable to be trusted. So correctness and performance verification are free, always.
P2 — Local-first is free. VERTO's moat is local, private, inspectable. Anything on the user's own hardware costs us nothing at the margin, builds adoption and trust, and is the differentiator. Giving it away is the strategy. Covers the CLI, the rule library, the local LLM, and the self-run CI Action (it runs in your CI, on your compute).
P3 — Premium = hosted value with real marginal cost. Charge only where we spend real money and add real value: our compute (a managed model, clean-room benchmarking) or our infrastructure (team dashboards, shared history, org policy). Genuine cost per use, genuine convenience/quality upside — fair, and honest.
P4 — Gating is architectural, never a feature-flag in the client. There is no if premium: in the open client. The free client is fully functional alone. A premium capability is a service call gated by a verto-token + server-side entitlement — a flag that needs the server simply fails without a token, it is not "present but disabled." This stops the "just patch the OSS client" crack and keeps the free tier honest.

2The test you apply to any new flag

"Does this run entirely on the user's machine, or does it call VERTO's servers?"  Local → free.  Server → premium (needs a verto-token).

Every classification below is just this question, applied.

3FREE — the whole local product FREE

Every command, and every flag that acts locally — ~95% of the surface.

Commands (all free)

CommandWhy free
verto analyzeInspect opportunities locally — no writes, no server.
verto optimizeThe core loop, runs on your machine.
verto reportReads your local ledger.
verto initSets up the local .verto/ workspace.
verto serveA warm local daemon — your process, your socket.

The gate & verification — P1, never paywalled

--min-rung --min-speedup --objectives --fuzz --seed --fp-tolerance --metamorphic --reps --reps-min --no-adaptive --fast

These decide whether a change is accepted. Paywalling any of them would paywall the proof. Off the table. (--fast is the local sound-vs-speed toggle — it skips the sanitizer rung on request and loudly labels the result unsound; still local, still free.)

Proposers — rules & local LLM (P2)

--model rules --model local --offline --llm-model --llm-url --candidates --refine --no-cache

Rules run locally; the local LLM runs on your box (Ollama) — your source never leaves. The best-so-far cache is local state.

Reach oracles — your tests & profile

--test-command --test-dir --test-timeout --bench-command --bench-dir --bench-runs --build-command --ctest-dir --profile

These point VERTO at your test suite / profile on your machine. Nothing hosted.

Apply, output, patches

--apply --dry-run --diff --emit-patches --backup --force --apply-from --json --export --quiet --stop

Writing a verified change to your own source, or emitting a patch series, is local. Apply is never paywalled — a verified win you can't take is pointless.

Scale, sandbox, budget-meter

--changed --jobs --no-sandbox --sandbox-mem --budget --budget-per-hotspot

Git-scoping, local parallelism, the local sandbox, and the local spend-meter (it just counts — it doesn't call us).

CI (self-run) & setup

--fail-on --compile-commands --verify-setup --list-transforms --transforms --pull --config --config-file --no-daemon --no-color --all --global --version

The self-run GitHub Action is free — it runs in your CI on your runners. Setup/inspection commands are local.

5The gray areas (called out honestly)

FlagRulingWhy
--model frontierFree (BYO key)You bring your own OpenAI-compatible endpoint + key (an env var). It never touches our servers → free. Premium only when pointed at VERTO's managed endpoint (that's --model hosted).
--budget / --budget-per-hotspotFree (as a meter)Counting/limiting spend is local. It only bills when the spend is on our managed model — and that billing is the premium part, not the flag.
The GitHub ActionFree to self-runRuns in your CI on your runners. Premium only in hosted mode (our compute + dashboard).
--profileFreeConsumes your perf.data. A hosted profiler would be a separate premium capability, not this flag.

The pattern: the flag is usually free; the hosted backend it can point at is what's paid. That keeps the client honest and un-cracked (P4).

6What we commit to NEVER paywall

  1. The gate — accept ⟺ correct ∧ faster. The proof is free.
  2. --apply — taking a verified win into your own code.
  3. Rules + the local LLM — proposing, fully offline.
  4. The self-run CI Action — VERTO in your pipeline.
  5. The CLI itself — analyze / optimize / report / init / serve.

If a future flag would paywall any of these, it's the wrong flag.

7One-line summary for the pitch

VERTO is free where it runs on your machine — the whole optimizer, the whole proof. You pay only for our servers doing what your laptop can't: a managed model, clean-room timing, and the team layer.
